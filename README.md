## How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I have learned to ask what someone is trying to do before deciding how to build it. During the SNHU Travel focus group, one customer wanted a list of popular destinations, but others wanted that list to reflect their budget, travel history, or preferred type of trip. Those are different needs, even though they all relate to choosing a vacation. Writing user stories helped me separate the requests and explain who needed each feature, what they wanted, and why it mattered. It also showed me where I needed more information. For example, a request for personalized destinations does not explain how the recommendations should be ranked. That is something the Product Owner, developer, and tester would need to work through together.

## How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I like to understand the requirement first, break the work into smaller pieces, and test what I build before moving on. In this course, I worked with a basic Java destination list and then a slideshow that needed different navigation checks. When the Product Owner changed the direction to wellness travel, the existing slideshow did not need to be thrown out. The content could change while the working navigation stayed in place. That made the value of Agile much clearer to me. I would like to keep using backlog refinement to clear up requirements, Sprint Planning to set realistic goals, Daily Scrums to surface problems early, and Sprint Reviews and Retrospectives to learn from the results. I also want to be more careful about defining what "done" actually means before calling a feature complete.

## What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

To me, a good team member does their part but also pays attention to what the rest of the team needs. A developer should ask questions when a story is unclear instead of guessing. A tester should explain a problem with steps others can reproduce. A Product Owner needs to explain priorities and be open to feedback, and the Scrum Master helps everyone work together rather than just handing out orders. I would rather raise a blocker early and help find a solution than stay quiet and let it become a bigger issue. Being dependable, listening when someone has a different perspective, and admitting when something still needs work are all part of being someone others can count on.

## What I am taking away from CS 250

The biggest lesson for me is that writing the code is only one part of building useful software. The team also has to understand customers, agree on requirements, communicate changes, test the result, and be willing to adjust. The SNHU Travel project showed me why that matters, especially when the business changes its priorities in the middle of development.
